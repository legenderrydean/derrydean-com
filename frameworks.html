The Sovereign Architect

Twelve named frameworks, one operating discipline.

Formal published research and essay-native diagnostic concepts, indexed in one place for the first time. Some were built for institutions and carry a paper and a workbook. Others were named in an essay and tested against a real audit finding. All of them are doing the same job: naming a mechanism precisely enough that it can be recognised the next time it appears.

Most of what follows was never meant to sit in one place. The formal frameworks, IMVCM, RISCAM, GIRS, were built inside funded research programmes, with co-investigators and citations. The essay-native ones, the Commissioning Trap, the Selection Trap, the Diffusion Trap, the private sovereign, regulatory market-creation, the permission tax, were named in the course of arguing something else, and only became reusable once the argument was finished. This page treats them as one body of work rather than two, because in practice they are: the same habit of looking for the mechanism underneath the outcome, applied at different altitudes and with different degrees of institutional backing.

Digital & Financial Infrastructure

4 frameworks

GIDTB — Ghana Integrated Digital Transformation Blueprint

Formal

A cabinet-level national blueprint naming where Ghana's digital transformation was going and why, rather than leaving the sequence of investment to improvisation. World Bank-backed, unlocking approximately $200M.

Role: Principal Architect, Ministry of Communications & Digitalisation
Read: The Blueprint Trilogy · CV

Ghana–Singapore Financial Trust Corridor

Formal

A bilateral digital financial interoperability framework between the Monetary Authority of Singapore and the Bank of Ghana, built so two national payment systems can recognise each other's rails rather than requiring a new integration for every counterparty.

Role: Chair, Technology Track
Read: About · CV

Regulatory Market-Creation

Essay-native

A regulator can constitute a market before any single actor is positioned to capture it, if the sequence of rules arrives ahead of the incumbents rather than in response to them. The mechanism behind Ghana becoming a market processing an estimated $384.7bn a year.

Introduced in: The $384.7 Billion Architecture
Applied in: the 2008 Branchless Banking Guidelines through the 2019 Payment Systems and Services Act

The Permission Tax

Essay-native

The portion of a founding team's time and attention consumed by institutional navigation, licences, approvals, regulatory relationships, rather than by building the product itself. A hidden line item in every founding story in a regulated market.

Introduced in: The $384.7 Billion Architecture
Grounded in: DreamOval's founding, 2008–2019

Innovation & Research Commercialisation

4 frameworks

IMVCM — Innovation Markets & Value Chain Map

Formal

A 14-node diagnostic protocol across five phases tracing the distance between a research idea and a product in daily use, research intention, funding, development, IP protection, prototyping, investor matching, licensing, product development, conformity assessment, and go-to-market, with a gate at every node.

Co-authored with Prof. Gordon Adomdza, UK-Ghana Innovation Trust Corridor
Read: Published paper & workbook · applied in The Commissioning Trap

RISCAM

Formal

Research, Innovation, Socialisation, Communication, Advocacy and Mainstreaming: the operational framework for how a research output actually reaches public awareness and adoption, rather than stopping at publication.

Co-investigators: Dr. Abena A. Yeboah-Banin, Dr. George Acheampong, Prof. Clement K.I. Appah
Read: Published blueprint

GIRS — Global Innovation Readiness Standard

Formal

A proposed global standard and assessment framework for innovation readiness, built on the IMVCM methodology, positioned to let institutions in different countries score against the same diagnostic rather than each inventing their own.

Read: Track Record

Innovation Trust Corridor (ITC)

Formal

A cross-border research-to-market exchange model, proposed as a five-country commercialisation concept spanning Ghana, Kenya, Uganda, Nigeria, and South Africa, under UK Government funding.

Read: Research-based evidence & policy paper

Industrial & Institutional Policy

4 frameworks

The Commissioning Trap

Essay-native

A policy that measures its own success at the ribbon-cutting rather than at the plant gate, treating the existence of a building as the achievement rather than the beginning of one. Traced across four Ghanaian governments and seventy years, and against a December 2025 independent audit estimating $2.6–2.9bn a year in lost revenue from underutilised flagship factories.

Introduced in: The Commissioning Trap (Archive Revival, publishes 17 Aug 2026)

The Selection Trap

Essay-native

The reflex to treat three complementary layers of state-led innovation policy, the champion model, the enclave model, the hub model, as competing brands, then commit an entire national budget to whichever one is fashionable in that year's donor conversation, instead of sequencing all three.

Introduced in: The Sequencing Argument, The Blueprint Trilogy

The Diffusion Trap

Essay-native

What happens when a government funds "digital transformation" in general without naming the specific sector it intends to own: infrastructure spending arrives everywhere in a diluted form and matters nowhere in a decisive one.

Introduced in: The Sequencing Argument, The Blueprint Trilogy

The Private Sovereign

Essay-native

What a government has actually built when it anoints a single champion firm with no rivals: not a national champion, but a company that controls an entire domain, including the workforce that depends on it, and answers to no electorate and no regulator.

Introduced in: The Sequencing Argument, The Blueprint Trilogy
Applying This

If one of these names your problem

These are diagnostic tools before they are essays. If a programme, a platform, or a policy you're responsible for looks like it's heading into one of these patterns, that's a conversation worth having before the ribbon gets cut, not after.
